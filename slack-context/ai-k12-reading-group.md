## JACK MOUNTJOY — HISD context + design (verbatim, the key message)
> "There are several waves of interventions happening in HISD thanks to the state takeover of the district... superintendent Mike Miles launched his 'New Education System' (NES) in Fall 2023, which is centered around direct instruction and has some No Excuses charter parallels, at 85 of the district's 260 schools. More schools have been added each year, now around half of the district. That NES variation alone would be interesting to evaluate.
>
> 'Future 2', the Alpha-esque model, is the latest wave... initially targeting just two campuses for Fall 2026 (Gregg & Clemente Martinez) but recently expanded to nine, though Gregg & Clemente Martinez seem to be specially denoted as the 'flagship' Future 2 schools. Note that Alpha is never publicly mentioned in connection with Future 2, perhaps to avoid backlash...
>
> ...we probably want to broaden the focus from solely a DiD of the two flagship schools vs. the other seven Future 2 schools. That's still useful, specifically for isolating the effect of Timeback among the eligible kids. But for a more 'Alpha vs. non-Alpha' comparison, I think we'd want to compare these Future 2 schools to non-Future 2 schools, perhaps NES vs. non-NES separately as relevant comparison groups, and exploit the staggered roll-out from the initial 2 flagships to the other 7 to more in the future.
>
> I also noticed that the two Future 2 flagship schools have their own application form, with spots currently being offered on a first-come, first-served basis. It would be useful if they could continue to accumulate applications... beyond the initial capacity, generating another useful comparison group: interested families who didn't initially get a seat. The seven non-flagship Future 2 schools use the regular Houston application system, which may also generate useful variation from the centralized Houston assignment mechanism (which includes lotteries)."

Key HISD facts from Jack:
- HISD under **state takeover**; Supt **Mike Miles**.
- **NES** (New Education System) — direct instruction, No-Excuses parallels, Fall 2023, now ~half of 260 schools. *NES variation alone is evaluable.*
- **Future 2** = the Alpha-esque model. Fall 2026. **2 flagships (Gregg & Clemente Martinez) → expanded to 9.** Alpha NOT publicly named (backlash avoidance).
- **Timeback** = the within-Future-2 treatment.
- Design menu: (a) DiD 2 flagships vs 7 other Future 2 = isolates Timeback among eligible; (b) Future 2 vs non-Future 2, NES vs non-NES as separate comparison groups; (c) staggered rollout 2→7→more; (d) flagship first-come-first-served application → "interested but didn't get seat" control group; (e) 7 non-flagships use centralized Houston assignment (has lotteries) → lottery variation.

## JACK MOUNTJOY — TEFA voucher (verbatim, two messages)
Msg 1:
> "I dug into the TEFA private school voucher program... the voucher lottery did not seem to have families provide any pre-lottery preferences over particular private schools. There does seem to be some nice lottery variation within Tier 2 families (51,000 awarded, 20,000 currently waitlisted), but the treatment here is simply getting the voucher... Given Alpha's tiny current share of the overall private school market in Texas, we shouldn't expect the TEFA lottery variation alone to be useful for identifying effects of enrolling at Alpha specifically; that first stage will likely be tiny. Instead, the Alpha-specific opportunity here seems to lie with how Alpha will ration the demand for their campuses among TEFA-awarded families. If far more awarded families want to use their TEFA voucher at an Alpha campus than they have seats, then we should urge Alpha to randomize those offers... My guess is that many Alpha 'losers' will end up in other private schools, and thus not the public school admin data, but we could aim to measure future outcomes like AP test taking, AP scores, SAT/ACT scores, and NSC college enrollment. One nice administrative advantage... it would run directly from Alpha internal data to outcomes: Alpha would have the list of all interested TEFA-awardees, the random assignments into offers vs. non-offers, and enrollment data for the takers..."

Msg 2 (addenda):
> "The voucher application platform (run by Odyssey...) actually DID allow parents to list a particular private school target prior to the lottery, but this was optional and didn't have any bearing on the lottery process. If sufficient numbers of parents did so and listed an Alpha campus, then there could be hope of a meaningful first stage of random voucher offer -> Alpha enrollment among that subsample.
>
> There is hope of measuring shorter-term test score outcomes: 'TEFA program participants in grades 3-12 enrolled in an approved private school are required to take an approved nationally norm-referenced or state-approved assessment each year... Parents are required to provide assessment results to Odyssey...'
>
> So, another research proposal... see whether sufficient numbers of parents optionally listed Alpha prior to the lottery, and if so, lobby the TEFA authorities to have Odyssey share data... which would include the TEFA applications, optional pre-lottery school preference, lottery results, enrollment, and test score outcomes."

Jack follow-up on which campuses have excess demand:
> "the Alpha sports academies are likely to have the most excess demand from TEFA awardees, and maybe Brownsville too -- any campus with a tuition amount closer to the TEFA award, which is about $10,000. Given the low family incomes of the awardees, the $10,000 voucher is unlikely to boost their demand for the super expensive campuses."

Parag reply: "We probably should reach out to the Alpha team involved in these sports academies once we have an opening with Alpha."
